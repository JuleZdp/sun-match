How this is calculated

The sun starts as a roughly 5,900K source above the atmosphere. At lower angles its light passes through more air, which scatters blue out of the beam and warms it. Haze adds more scattering and also brightens the sky. Open shade is skylight plus light bounced off average ground (15% reflectance), measured on the side facing away from the sun.

Values assume sea level and are rounded to 50K. Treat them as a starting point: real days vary, most of all within 5° of the horizon. Swatches show how each light will look in camera.

Relative mode moves both lights by the same amount in mireds (1,000,000 ÷ Kelvin), the scale cameras and gels use, so equal steps look equally different. That keeps the warm sun and cool shade the same distance apart.
